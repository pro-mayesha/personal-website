# Writing Style Guide

Guidance for Mayesha Maliha Proma’s public essays and internal research notes.

---

## Medium essays (Short Blogs on AI & Technology)

Published on [Medium](https://mproma.medium.com). These are beginner-friendly public essays.

### Voice

- **Beginner-friendly** — assume curiosity, not prior expertise
- **Personal but purposeful** — use “I” when it helps learning, not for decoration
- **Curious rather than authoritative** — invite the reader to think with you
- **Technically accurate** — simplify without distorting the idea
- **Based on relatable examples** — tools, products, everyday analogies
- **Honest about learning** — say what was confusing and how it clicked
- **Practical rather than textbook-like** — connect concepts to building and experiments

### Scope

These essays explain technical ideas (data science, neural networks, language models, applied AI) in plain language. They are **not** research-note folders and should not be filed under Paper Notes or other notebook categories.

### On the website

- Show title, optional subtitle, date, reading time, short excerpt, tags, and an external Medium link
- Do **not** copy full Medium article bodies onto the site
- Prefer live RSS (`https://mproma.medium.com/feed`) with a verified local fallback

## Research notes (AI & Education Reflections)

Internal notebook at `/notes`. Umbrella title: **AI & Education Reflections**.

### Folders

1. **Paper Notes** — readings and methodological takeaways  
2. **Research Questions** — open questions worth carrying forward  
3. **Field Experiment Ideas** — early testable ideas in real products  
4. **General Reflections** — broader connections across learning, products, and users  

Topics may include human-centered AI, AI-supported decisions, study-abroad platforms, trust, adoption, engagement, retention, agents, causal field experiments, and NLP systems — not only classroom education.

### Voice

- **More exploratory** than Medium essays
- May contain **unresolved questions**
- Clearly distinguish **evidence**, **interpretation**, and **speculation**
- **Reference** the paper or source being discussed
- Record **methodological concerns and limitations**
- Avoid presenting early ideas as **confirmed findings**
- **Preserve questions** rather than forcing conclusions
- Connect readings to **possible experiments** or product observations
- **Never invent** results, user data, or finished conclusions

### Publishing rules

- Fields: `title`, `slug`, `date`, `category`, `excerpt`, `tags`, `published`, `featured`, `status`
- Categories: `paper-notes` | `research-questions` | `field-experiment-ideas` | `reflections`
- Statuses: `draft` | `developing` | `published`
- Only show full note cards when `published: true`
- Keep folder cards and empty states visible even with zero published notes
- Empty-state copy: “Notes will be added here as the research develops.”

### Separation from Medium

| Form | Home | Tone |
|------|------|------|
| Short blogs | Medium (RSS on academic page) | Teaching / learning in public |
| Research notebook | `/notes` | Exploratory / unfinished thinking |

Do not merge Medium articles into the research-notes folders. Do not label Medium essays as paper notes, or internal reflections as Medium blogs.
